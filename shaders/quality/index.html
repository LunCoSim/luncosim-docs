    2. 7 Shaders

    Quality¶

    Rendering quality is a budget choice, not a shader-identity switch. The RenderingQualitySettings resource owns budgets that affect all GPU-backed views. USD remains the authority for the scene's ShaderLook and authored WESL source.

    A scene can therefore keep its authored lunar terrain and starfield shaders while the High preset supplies the largest renderer budgets around them.

    Presets¶

    Preset Intent
    Low Lowest-cost shadow and lighting budgets
    Balanced The default interactive balance
    High Largest shipped shadow, lighting, derived-map, rock, and tessellation budgets

    The stable policy keys are low, balanced, and high. The Graphics settings menu presents the same three choices. The CLI can select one for a process:

    luncosim --render-quality high

    The setting is persisted with the other graphics settings. It is not a per-material property and it does not replace the shader authored by the scene.

    What the preset budgets¶

    RenderingQualitySettings covers:

    Family Examples
    Shadow maps Directional, point, and spot map sizes; cascade count; caster limits; shadow storage budget
    Shadow filtering Hardware 2×2 or Gaussian 5×5
    Horizon cache Enablement, sun-angle invalidation threshold, march steps, bake supersampling
    Camera MSAA, tone mapping, exposure, bloom
    Sky Dome cubemap face size
    Terrain LOD Tile resolution, pixel error, maximum depth, tile budget, bake cadence, hysteresis, morph band
    Terrain derived maps Roughness/AO/normal resolution, AO directions and steps, anisotropy
    Terrain rocks Maximum instances, mesh buckets, LOD fade distances
    Geometry Sphere, cylinder, capsule, NURBS, and curve tessellation
    Editor preview Preview key light, local-light defaults, render-failure recovery

    The exact numeric values belong to the shipped profile and may change between releases. Read the current settings in Settings → Graphics rather than copying a table from a version of the documentation.

    Shadow filtering¶

    The Graphics settings expose two shadow filters:

    Filter Character
    Hardware 2×2 Four comparison samples with hardware filtering; lower cost
    Gaussian 5×5 A wider nine-sample footprint; highest quality without temporal anti-aliasing

    The default is Gaussian. The terrain material's normal bias is scaled by the shadow texel size so grazing-angle terrain does not sample its own surface inside the filter footprint.

    Terrain quality¶

    The High preset keeps the interactive terrain CDLOD envelope at the proven Balanced bound: 49-vertex tiles, depth 8, a 768-tile cover, and the corresponding sampling and bake cadence. High increases the surrounding lighting, derived-map, rock, and tessellation budgets; it does not double terrain cover merely to spend the frame budget on duplicate geometry.

    Per-tile texture quality is authored separately. A near tile can use a 2048² albedo and a far tile a 256² one, with the same roles and different images. This is the right way to spend quality where the camera actually looks.

    Quality does not change material identity¶

    The quality preset does not rewrite a ShaderLook. It changes renderer budgets around the authored material. A material that is invalid at Low is still invalid at High; a shader that fails to compile is a shader error, not a quality setting.

    Likewise, changing a quality setting does not repair a missing texture or a malformed parameter. Those are authoring errors and remain visible.

    Measuring cost¶

    A deterministic recording run is not a profiling run. Use a separate session when measuring frame time:

    luncosim --no-vsync --no-throttle --log-diag

    --log-diag reports FPS, frame time, and physics diagnostics. Compare presets in the same scene, camera, and resolution; a different view invalidates the comparison.

    For a headless gate, keep the deterministic --threads 1 --jitter 0 profile. Graphics assertions are a separate serial offscreen pass because GPU state and driver behavior are not part of the fixed-step determinism contract.

    Material sharing and quality¶

    Identical looks share one material and one bind group. The look key includes texture handles, so two tiles at different texture resolutions correctly get two materials, while tiles at the same quality share one.

    Varying a scalar per instance mints a material per instance. If a quality choice must vary continuously, bucket it into a small set of values rather than giving every instance a unique float.
